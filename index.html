How it works

Three steps each way. No accounts, no servers, no waiting.

STEP 01

Load your image

Drag a file, pick one from disk, or paste straight from the clipboard. The image never leaves your browser.

STEP 02

Optimise & encode

The image is downscaled and JPEG-compressed until it fits the QR payload budget, then packed into the code.

STEP 03

Scan or decode

Scan the QR with any phone, or feed it back into the decoder to restore the picture and download it.

Frequently asked questions

The short answers to the things people actually ask.

How can an entire image fit inside a QR code?

It doesn't fit at full resolution. A QR code tops out at roughly 2.9 KB of binary data at the lowest error-correction level. QRForge downscales and JPEG-compresses your image until the encoded data URI fits inside that budget — think of it as a thumbnail, not a print-quality copy.

Why does the recovered image look lower quality?

Because the QR format has a hard size limit. To keep it scannable, the image is reduced to a small resolution and re-encoded as a compressed JPEG. Raising the detail slider gives you more pixels, but the resulting QR becomes denser and harder for older phone cameras to read.

Are my images uploaded anywhere?

No. Every step — reading the file, resizing, JPEG encoding, QR generation and QR decoding — runs in your browser using standard JavaScript and Canvas APIs. You can even disconnect from the network after the page loads and everything still works.

What happens if the QR code is too dense to scan?

Lower the Detail setting to Compact, keep error correction on L, and display the QR on a bright, high-contrast screen. Scanning directly off a monitor usually works better than scanning a printed copy at small sizes.

Can I decode a QR code made by another app?

Yes. The decoder reads any QR code. If the payload is an image data URI it renders the picture; if it's a URL or plain text, you'll see the raw content instead.

Which image formats are supported?

Anything your browser can render: PNG, JPEG, WebP, GIF (first frame), BMP and SVG. Transparent areas are flattened onto a white background before compression.
